Ask About This Law
TL;DR
Zomato must tell you what data it keeps — and delete it when you ask.
The DPDP Act 2023 controls how apps like Zomato, Swiggy, and WhatsApp collect and store your personal data. When you use these apps, the company becomes a "data fiduciary" — they have legal duties to protect what you share with them.

Key Provisions
§4
Consent must be specific and withdrawable
Zomato can only use your location with your explicit consent. If you delete your account, they must erase your data within 30 days.
Section 4(1): "A data fiduciary shall process personal data only for the purpose for which the data principal has given consent."
§7
You can see and correct your data
You can request any app to show all data they hold about you, correct wrong entries, or delete it entirely.
Section 7(a): "Every data principal shall have the right to obtain from the data fiduciary a summary of personal data being processed."
Impact on Farmer
⚠ Not Directly Applicable
The DPDP Act primarily covers digital services. As a farmer doing offline agricultural work, this law does not affect you unless you use apps like AgroStar, the PM Kisan portal, or digital banking.
If you use Aadhaar-linked government schemes, those platforms' data handling is covered.

Right to written notice before deactivation
CLEAR ✓ VERIFIED
The platform must give written notice before deactivating you or cutting your pay. You have a right to be heard before such decisions take effect.
Section 43(1) — Code on Social Security 2020: "An aggregator shall not terminate the engagement of any gig worker without giving him a notice period and the reason for termination."
Right to social security scheme enrollment
LIKELY ✓ VERIFIED
Gig workers may be enrolled in government social security schemes covering life insurance, disability insurance, and health benefits.
Section 109(1) — Code on Social Security 2020: "The Central Government may frame social security schemes for gig workers and platform workers."
Right to a grievance redressal mechanism
UNCERTAIN ⚠ UNVERIFIED
The law mandates a grievance mechanism, but no specific procedure has been notified by the government. The right exists in statute but is not yet operational.
Section 43(3): "Every aggregator shall establish a grievance redressal mechanism for gig workers."
What the Law Does Not Cover
  • Statutory minimum earnings guarantee — no floor wage for gig workers yet
  • Reinstatement rights — you cannot force the platform to re-enroll you
  • Liability for arbitrary algorithm-driven decisions

◎ Scope Overlap Regulatory jurisdiction over telecom data
DPDP Act 2023
Classifies telecom operators as data fiduciaries subject to all DPDP provisions.
§2(i): "data fiduciary means any person who… determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data."
Telecom Act 2023
Grants DoT authority over all telecom data, creating conflicting regulatory jurisdiction.
§4(2): "Every telecommunication entity shall comply with the data protection provisions as specified by the Authority."
≈ Definitional Conflict Definition of "personal data" vs. "message"
DPDP Act 2023
"Personal data" includes any data that can identify a natural person — broad definition covering call records, SMS content, IP addresses.
§2(t): "personal data means any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data."
Telecom Act 2023
"Message" is defined narrowly as telecommunication signals — may exclude metadata and usage patterns from its own data protections.
§2(p): "message means any sign, signal, writing, image, sound, intelligence or information transmitted through telecommunication."
